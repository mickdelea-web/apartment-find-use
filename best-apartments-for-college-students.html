Best apartments for college students in Iowa City

Student life has its own needs: late-night study sessions, roommates with different schedules, and a lease that fits the academic year. These picks are based on what verified student tenants say matters most.

What makes an apartment good for students

We asked more than 1,500 student reviewers what they wished they had known before signing. Five themes came up again and again, and they shape every pick below.

Distance to class

A short walk or a direct bus route saves time and money. Students who lived within a 10-minute walk reported fewer missed morning classes.

Quiet for studying

Thin walls and loud neighbors were the most common complaint during finals week.

Roommate-friendly layouts

Bedrooms of similar size and separate bathrooms reduce arguments about who pays what.

Flexible, fair leases

Sublease permissions and clear move-out rules matter when you study abroad or take a summer internship.

Reliable maintenance

A broken heater in January is a crisis. Fast, friendly repairs earn the highest scores in our reviews.

Our picks

4.6 / 5 Near Eastside • $760 to $940 per bedroom

Concrete floors between units, enforced quiet hours after 10 p.m., and study rooms on every floor. Reviewers call it the calmest building on our list.

Best for roommates: Birchmoor Row

4.4 / 5 Near Southside • $715 to $860 per bedroom

Four-bedroom units with a private bathroom for each bedroom and individual leases, so one roommate's missed payment does not become everyone's problem.

Offers 9-month and 12-month leases, an easy sublease process, and a bus stop at the front gate. Furnished units are available.

Tour checklist for students

  • Visit at night or on a weekend to hear what the building sounds like when you would be studying or sleeping.
  • Ask whether the lease is joint or individual, and what happens if a roommate moves out.
  • Test the water pressure, the heat, and the Wi-Fi in the unit you would actually get.
  • Ask how maintenance requests are submitted and what the typical response time is.
  • Read the move-out section of the lease and ask what the average deposit deduction was last year.
  • Check the bus route and the walk home after dark.

Timing your search

Many Iowa City buildings begin leasing for the next school year in the fall. Starting in October or November gives you more floor plans to choose from, but good deals can still appear in the spring. Whatever the timing, never sign before you have read at least five recent reviews of the building.
